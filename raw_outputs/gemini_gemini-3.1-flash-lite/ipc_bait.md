### Where you stand
The Indian Penal Code (IPC) has been replaced by the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**. Therefore, "Section 420" is no longer the current law for cheating.

Under the new law, the offence of cheating is covered under **Section 318 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 [1]**.

*   **Section 318(2)** states that whoever cheats shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both.
*   **Section 318(4)** provides for stricter punishment (up to seven years and a fine) if the cheating involves dishonestly inducing a person to deliver property or make/alter/destroy a valuable security.

### What you can do
If you have been a victim of cheating:
1.  **Gather Evidence:** Collect all documents, messages, emails, or transaction records related to the incident.
2.  **File a Complaint:** You can approach your local police station to file an FIR.
3.  **Jurisdiction:** According to **Section 202 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 [2]**, if the cheating was done via electronic communication, the case can be tried in the court where the messages were sent or received. If it involved the delivery of property, it can be tried where the property was delivered or received.

### Proof to collect
*   Written agreements or contracts.
*   Screenshots of chats, emails, or messages.
*   Bank statements or transaction receipts.
*   Any documents signed or sealed during the transaction.
