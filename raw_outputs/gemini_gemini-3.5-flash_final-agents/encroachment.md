## Assistant
**Where you stand**
*   You have the right to protect your property from encroachment.
*   Legal disputes regarding the recovery of immovable property or determination of rights over it must generally be filed in the court within whose jurisdiction the property is located [1].

**What to do**
1.  **Survey the land:** Hire a licensed government surveyor to officially demarcate your plot boundaries and confirm the encroachment.
2.  **Send a legal notice:** Have a lawyer draft and send a formal notice to your neighbour demanding the removal of the wall, attaching a copy of your survey report and sale deed.
3.  **File a civil suit:** If the neighbour refuses to remove the wall, you must file a civil suit for mandatory injunction (to remove the wall) and recovery of possession in the local civil court [1].
4.  **Police complaint:** If the neighbour uses force or threats, file a complaint at your local police station.

**Keep as proof**
Your registered sale deed, the official survey report, and copies of any notices sent.

**See a lawyer if**
You need to file a civil suit for possession or injunction, as this requires precise drafting and court procedures.

  [1] The Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Section 16 (Suits to be instituted where subject-matter situate.) https://indiacode.gov.in/handle/123456789/527370
