Builder promised flat possession in 2022, it is still not ready. I paid 30 lakh. Can I get my money back with interest?

### 1. What I understood
You booked a flat and paid 30 lakhs to a builder. The possession was promised by 2022, but the project is still not ready. You now want to withdraw from the project and get your money back with interest.

### 2. Where you stand
Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA), if a promoter (builder) fails to complete or is unable to give possession of an apartment by the date specified in the agreement for sale, you have the right to withdraw from the project.

According to **[1]**, if you choose to withdraw, the builder is liable to return the amount you paid, along with interest and compensation. Alternatively, if you do not wish to withdraw, you are entitled to receive interest for every month of delay until possession is handed over **[1]**.

### 3. What you can do
1.  **Review your Agreement:** Check your "Agreement for Sale" to confirm the exact possession date and the terms regarding delays.
2.  **Send a Formal Notice:** Write a formal letter/email to the builder stating that the possession date has passed and you are exercising your right to withdraw and claim a refund with interest under RERA.
3.  **File a Complaint with RERA:** If the builder does not respond or refuses to pay, you can file a formal complaint with the Real Estate Regulatory Authority (RERA) in your state. Most states have an online portal for this.
4.  **Legal Aid:** If you need help drafting the complaint or navigating the RERA portal, you can contact the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) helpline at **15100** for free legal advice.

### 4. Proof to collect
*   **Agreement for Sale:** The signed document between you and the builder.
*   **Payment Receipts:** Bank statements, receipts, or transaction IDs showing the 30 lakh payment.
*   **Communication:** Copies of emails, letters, or messages where the builder promised possession or acknowledged the delay.
*   **Project Details:** Any brochures or documents mentioning the promised possession date.

### 5. When to get a lawyer
You should consult a lawyer if:
*   The builder refuses to acknowledge your claim or threatens you.
*   The builder has filed for insolvency (bankruptcy).
*   You are unsure how to calculate the interest or compensation amount for your RERA complaint.
