### 1. What I understood
You received a cheque for Rs 50,000 from a friend to repay a loan, but the cheque has bounced (dishonoured).

### 2. Where you stand
Under the law, if a cheque is dishonoured due to insufficient funds, the person who issued the cheque (the drawer) is liable to compensate the holder, provided certain legal steps are followed [1], [2].

For this to be a legally enforceable claim, the debt must be a "legally enforceable debt or other liability" [2].

### 3. What you can do
If you wish to pursue this, the process generally involves these steps:
1.  **Get the Memo:** Ensure you have the "Cheque Return Memo" from your bank, which explains why the cheque was dishonoured (e.g., "Funds Insufficient").
2.  **Send a Legal Notice:** You must send a formal notice in writing to your friend within 30 days of receiving the information about the cheque bounce from your bank. This notice must demand the payment of the cheque amount.
3.  **Wait for Payment:** Your friend has 15 days from the date they receive your notice to pay the amount [2].
4.  **File a Complaint:** If they do not pay within those 15 days, you can file a criminal complaint in the appropriate court.

### 4. Proof to collect
*   The original cheque that bounced.
*   The bank's "Cheque Return Memo" (the document stating the reason for the bounce).
*   A copy of the legal notice you send to your friend.
*   Proof of delivery of the legal notice (e.g., registered post receipt/acknowledgment).
*   Any documents proving the original loan (e.g., bank transfer records, loan agreement, or messages/emails discussing the loan).

### 5. When to get a lawyer
You should consult a lawyer immediately to draft the legal notice. It is crucial that the notice is legally sound and sent within the strict 30-day timeline. If the notice is not sent correctly or on time, you may lose your right to file a complaint under Section 138.
