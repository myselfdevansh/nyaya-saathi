I am 72. My son took my savings and now refuses to give me money for medicines. Can I do anything?
## Assistant
I am not a lawyer, but I can explain the legal options available to you under the law.

**What I understood**
You are a 72-year-old senior citizen. Your son has taken your savings and is now refusing to provide financial support for your medical needs.

**Where you stand**
Under the *Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007*, you have a legal right to claim maintenance from your children if you are unable to support yourself [1]. 

The law states that children have an obligation to maintain their parents so that they can lead a normal life [1]. If your son is neglecting or refusing to provide this support, a Tribunal can order him to pay a monthly maintenance allowance [2].

**What you can do**
1.  **File an Application:** You can file an application for maintenance before the Maintenance Tribunal in your district [3].
2.  **Seek Legal Aid:** Since this can be a difficult process, you can contact the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) helpline at **15100**. They provide free legal assistance to senior citizens.
3.  **Enforcement:** If the Tribunal passes an order for maintenance and your son still does not pay, the order can be enforced by the Tribunal [4].

**Proof to collect**
*   Any documents or bank statements showing the transfer of your savings to your son.
*   Medical prescriptions or bills that prove your need for financial support for medicines.
*   Any written communication (messages, emails, or letters) where you asked for support and he refused.

**When to get a lawyer**
You should consult a lawyer or a legal aid clinic immediately to help you draft the application for the Tribunal. While you can file the application yourself, having legal guidance will ensure the paperwork is correct and your rights are protected.

**To help me give you better information, could you please clarify:**
1.  In which state and city are you currently residing?
2.  Did you transfer any property (house or land) to your son on the condition that he would take care of you? (This is important because the law has specific provisions for voiding such transfers if the condition is not met [5]).
