I am not a lawyer, and I cannot provide legal representation. However, I can explain the rights of an arrested person based on the laws available to me.

### 1. What I understood
Your brother was arrested by the police last night, and you have not been informed of the reason for his arrest or his current status.

### 2. Where you stand
Under the law, the police have specific duties when they arrest someone. They cannot simply hold a person without following procedure.

*   **Right to know the reason:** Every police officer arresting someone without a warrant must immediately inform the person of the full details of the offense or the grounds for the arrest [1].
*   **Right to inform family/friends:** The police must inform a relative, friend, or any person nominated by the arrested person about the arrest and the place where they are being held [2].
*   **Right to meet a lawyer:** Your brother has the right to meet an advocate of his choice during interrogation [3].
*   **Right to be produced before a Court:** The police must bring the arrested person before a Magistrate without unnecessary delay, and this delay cannot exceed 24 hours (excluding travel time) [4].
*   **Arrest Procedure:** The officer must wear clear identification, prepare an arrest memo (signed by a witness and the arrested person), and inform the person of their right to have someone notified [5].

### 3. What you can do
1.  **Visit the Police Station:** Go to the police station immediately to ask for the "Arrest Memo." This document should contain the grounds for arrest and the details of the officer in charge.
2.  **Contact a Lawyer:** If the police refuse to provide information or if more than 24 hours have passed, you need a lawyer immediately to file a petition in court.
3.  **Legal Aid:** If you cannot afford a private lawyer, you can contact the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) helpline at **15100** for free legal assistance.
4.  **Emergency Help:** If you are in immediate danger or need to report police misconduct, you can call **112** (the national emergency number).

### 4. Proof to collect
*   Note down the name and badge number of the officers you speak to.
*   Keep a record of the time and date you visited the police station.
*   If possible, have a witness with you when you visit the station.

### 5. When to get a lawyer
You should get a lawyer immediately if:
*   More than 24 hours have passed since the arrest and your brother has not been produced before a Magistrate.
*   The police refuse to tell you where he is being held.
*   You suspect he is being mistreated or denied his rights.
