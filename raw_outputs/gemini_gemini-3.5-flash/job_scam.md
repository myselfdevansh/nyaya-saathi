### 1. What I understood
You paid Rs 40,000 to a person who promised to secure a railway job for you. After taking the money, his phone is switched off, and he cannot be reached.

---

### 2. Where you stand
Taking money by promising a job fraudulently is a serious criminal offence under Indian law:

* **Cheating:** Taking money by deceiving someone is cheating under Section 318 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023 [1]. If someone tricks you into handing over money by making false promises, it can carry a punishment of up to 7 years of imprisonment and a fine [1].
* **Personating a Public Servant:** If this person falsely claimed to be a railway officer or authority, Section 204 of the BNS applies for pretending to hold a public servant's office [2].
* **Compounding:** Under Section 359 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, cheating offences can be settled/compounded by the victim if the money is returned during legal proceedings [3].

---

### 3. What you can do

1. **File a Police Complaint immediately:** Visit the police station in the area where you paid the money or met the person [4]. Submit a written complaint detailing how you were cheated.
2. **Report Cybercrime (if paid online):** If you transferred money via UPI, net banking, or credit card, immediately report it on the National Cyber Crime Portal at **cybercrime.gov.in** or call the helpline **1930**.
3. **Contact Emergency Helpline:** Call **112** for general emergency police assistance.
4. **Free Legal Assistance:** If you need help preparing your complaint, call the NALSA free legal aid helpline at **15100**.

---

### 4. Proof to collect
Gather and organize the following evidence before going to the police:
* **Payment Proof:** Bank statements, UPI transaction receipts, or written cash receipts showing the Rs 40,000 transaction.
* **Communication Proof:** WhatsApp chats, SMS, call logs, emails, or audio recordings with the person.
* **Identity Details:** Phone numbers, photos, visiting cards, fake job offer letters, or fake railway application forms given by the man.

---

### 5. When to get a lawyer
You should consider hiring a lawyer (or applying for free legal aid through NALSA) if:
* The police refuse to register your complaint or FIR.
* You need to file a formal private criminal complaint before a Magistrate to direct police action.
