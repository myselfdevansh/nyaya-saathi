### 2. Where you stand
Under Indian law, your adult son is legally obligated to maintain you so that you can lead a normal life:

* **Right to Maintenance:** Under the *Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007*, a senior citizen (aged 60 or above) who cannot maintain themselves is entitled to claim maintenance from their adult children [maintenance_and_welfare_of_parents_and_senior_citizens_act_2007__s2, maintenance_and_welfare_of_parents_and_senior_citizens_act_2007__s4].
* **What Maintenance Includes:** "Maintenance" specifically covers food, clothing, residence, and medical attendance and treatment [1].
* **Monthly Allowance:** The Maintenance Tribunal can order your son to pay you a monthly maintenance allowance (up to ₹10,000 per month, depending on state rules) [2].
* **Property/Savings Transfer:** If you transferred money or property to your son on the condition that he would care for your basic needs, and he fails to do so, the Maintenance Tribunal can declare that transfer void [3].

---

### 3. What you can do (Step-by-Step)

1. **Call National Helpline for Senior Citizens (Elder Line):** Call **14567** (toll-free). They assist senior citizens in distress and guide you on filing maintenance applications local to your area.
2. **File an Application before the Maintenance Tribunal:** 
   * Approach the Maintenance Tribunal (usually headed by the Sub-Divisional Magistrate / SDM in your district).
   * Submit an application claiming monthly maintenance for medical expenses and living costs from your son.
3. **Contact Legal Aid (NALSA):** If you need help filling out forms or lodging the complaint, call the NALSA helpline at **15100** for free legal services.

---

### 4. Proof to collect
* **Age Proof:** Aadhaar card, PAN card, or passport showing you are 72 years old.
* **Medical Documents:** Doctor prescriptions, medical bills, and pharmacy receipts showing your health needs and monthly expense requirement.
* **Proof of Savings/Transfer:** Bank statements, passbooks, or withdrawal receipts showing money transferred to or taken by your son (if available).
* **Proof of Relationship:** Aadhaar, ration card, or birth certificate showing he is your adult son.

---

### 5. When to get a lawyer
You generally do not need a lawyer to file a case before the Maintenance Tribunal. However, you should consult a legal aid lawyer or legal practitioner if:
* Your son attempts to sell or transfer property/savings further.
* You need legal representation to recover a large amount of money stolen or fraudulently taken.
